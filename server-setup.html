Run your own AI server

Use a model on your own computer with Reword, for yourself or for family and friends. Windows 11 and Linux.

This guide uses Ollama (free software that runs AI models on your computer). Your messages go from your phone to your computer and nowhere else. Reword from Google Play only connects to servers over https://, so a plain address like http://192.168.1.20:11434 will not work; Step 3 gives you a secure address in one of two ways.

Do this at your own risk. These steps install software and change settings on your computer and, for Option B, on your router and your home internet connection. You are responsible for what you change. Reword and its makers are not responsible for any damage, lost data, security problems, costs or other harm that results. If you are not sure about a step, stop and ask someone who knows computers, or skip this guide and use the on-phone AI or a cloud AI instead.
Before you start
  • Your computer must be on and awake whenever you use Reword with it. If it sleeps, Reword cannot reach it.
  • You need a reasonably modern computer. 16 GB of memory is a good start, and a graphics card helps a lot. Without one, answers can take many seconds.
  • This uses other people's software (Ollama, Tailscale, Caddy). Their steps and menus change from time to time; if something here does not match what you see, their own websites are the final word.

Step 1: Install Ollama

Windows 11

Open Windows Terminal (right-click Start, then Terminal) and paste:

winget install Ollama.Ollama

No winget? Download the installer from ollama.com/download/windows.

Linux

curl -fsSL https://ollama.com/install.sh | sh

Step 2: Download a model

Pick one that fits your computer. The download is large, so use a fast connection.

  • Modest computer (8 GB of memory, no graphics card): gemma4:e2b (about 4.6 GB)
  • Most computers (16 GB of memory or a mid-range graphics card): gemma4:e4b (about 6.6 GB)
  • Strong graphics card (12 GB of video memory or more): gemma4:12b (about 8 GB)

Paste this, using the name you picked (this one is the middle choice), in Terminal on Windows or in a Linux terminal:

ollama pull gemma4:e4b

Check that it answers:

ollama run gemma4:e4b "Say hello in five words"

Type /bye to leave.

Step 3: Choose how your phone will reach it

Option A: just me
Easiest. Uses Tailscale (free). Nothing on your home network is opened to the internet. Each phone needs the Tailscale app.		 Option D: other people, with Tailscale
Share your computer with each person through Tailscale. No domain and no router changes. Each person needs the free Tailscale app and their own Tailscale account.
Option B: me and other people
Advanced. Uses a domain name, a free Let's Encrypt certificate and a separate key for each person. Needs router changes, and opens your home connection to the internet.		 Option C: at home only
Advanced. Works only while your phone is on your home Wi-Fi. Uses a free domain name and a free certificate, but no router changes and no Tailscale. Nothing is opened to the internet.

Pick one and follow only that part, then continue at Step 4. If you are not sure, choose A.

Option A: just me, with Tailscale

A1. Let Tailscale reach Ollama

By default Ollama listens only to its own computer and refuses requests that arrive under another name, which is how Tailscale delivers them. So we tell Ollama to listen on all of the computer's network addresses.

Warning: after this step, anyone on your home network (and anyone you let onto your Wi-Fi) can use your Ollama. It has no password. That is usually fine at home. Never forward a port on your router to it and never put it on the internet.

Windows 11

In Terminal:

setx OLLAMA_HOST "0.0.0.0:11434"

Then right-click the Ollama icon near the clock (you may need the little arrow to see it), choose Quit Ollama, and start Ollama again from the Start menu. If Windows asks whether to allow Ollama through the firewall, allow it on Private networks only.

Linux

sudo mkdir -p /etc/systemd/system/ollama.service.d
printf '[Service]\nEnvironment="OLLAMA_HOST=0.0.0.0:11434"\n' | sudo tee /etc/systemd/system/ollama.service.d/reword.conf
sudo systemctl daemon-reload
sudo systemctl restart ollama

A2. Install Tailscale and get an https address

  1. Make a free account at tailscale.com if you do not have one.
  2. Install Tailscale on your computer and sign in:

    Windows 11

    winget install Tailscale.Tailscale

    Then open Tailscale from the Start menu and sign in. Close and reopen Terminal afterwards.

    Linux

    curl -fsSL https://tailscale.com/install.sh | sh
sudo tailscale up

    Open the link it prints and sign in.

  3. Install the Tailscale app on your phone (Google Play), sign in with the same account and turn it on.
  4. Open login.tailscale.com/admin/dns, find HTTPS Certificates and click Enable HTTPS.
  5. On your computer, give Ollama its https address:

    Windows 11

    tailscale serve --bg 11434

    Linux

    sudo tailscale serve --bg 11434
    If it prints a link asking you to enable something, open it, approve, and run the command again.
  6. See your address:

    Windows 11

    tailscale serve status

    Linux

    sudo tailscale serve status
    It looks like https://my-computer.tail1234.ts.net. Copy it.

Check it from your phone: with Tailscale turned on, open that address in the phone's browser. It should say Ollama is running.

Note: the names of Tailscale devices can end up in public certificate records. Give your computer a name you do not mind being public.

Check it from your phone: with Tailscale turned on, open that address in the phone's browser. It should say Ollama is running. Then go on to Step 4.

Option B: me and other people, with a certificate and keys

Read this first, and remember: do this at your own risk.
  • This opens your home internet connection to the internet. The keys are passwords: anyone who has one can use your computer's AI until you remove it. There is no limit on how much each key can use, so give keys only to people you trust.
  • You need a domain name that points at your home internet address (a free one from duckdns.org works), and you must be able to forward ports 80 and 443 on your router. Some internet providers block those ports. If yours does, the certificate step fails; use Option A.
  • Here Ollama stays listening only to its own computer, which is safer than Option A. Do not do Option A's step A1 if you choose this option.

B1. A domain name that points to your home

Your domain's address record (an "A record") must hold your home's public internet address. Find it by searching the web for "what is my IP". If your provider changes it from time to time, use a service that updates it for you (duckdns.org has instructions). Then check it from your computer, replacing the example name with yours:

Windows 11

nslookup your-name.duckdns.org

Linux

getent hosts your-name.duckdns.org

The address it shows must be your home's public address.

B2. Open the ports

In your router's settings (often called Port Forwarding), forward TCP port 80 and TCP port 443 to your computer. Port 80 is only used by Let's Encrypt to check the domain is yours. In your computer's firewall:

Windows 11

In Terminal opened as administrator (right-click Terminal, Run as administrator):

New-NetFirewallRule -DisplayName "Reword Caddy" -Direction Inbound -Protocol TCP -LocalPort 80,443 -Action Allow

Linux

sudo ufw allow 80,443/tcp

(Only if you use ufw. If the command is not found, you have no ufw firewall to change.)

B3. Make a key for each person

Run this once for each person. It prints a 12-character key with no look-alike letters, easy to type on a phone. Write down who has which key.

Windows 11 (Terminal)

-join (1..12 | % { 'abcdefghjkmnpqrstuvwxyz23456789'[[System.Security.Cryptography.RandomNumberGenerator]::GetInt32(31)] })

Linux

LC_ALL=C tr -dc 'abcdefghjkmnpqrstuvwxyz23456789' < /dev/urandom | head -c 12; echo

B4. Install Caddy

Windows 11

winget install CaddyServer.Caddy

Close and reopen Terminal afterwards.

Linux (Debian and Ubuntu)

sudo apt install -y debian-keyring debian-archive-keyring apt-transport-https curl
curl -1sLf 'https://dl.cloudsmith.io/public/caddy/stable/gpg.key' | sudo gpg --dearmor -o /usr/share/keyrings/caddy-stable-archive-keyring.gpg
curl -1sLf 'https://dl.cloudsmith.io/public/caddy/stable/debian.deb.txt' | sudo tee /etc/apt/sources.list.d/caddy-stable.list
sudo apt update
sudo apt install caddy

Other Linux systems: see caddyserver.com/docs/install.

B5. The Caddy settings file

Make a file named Caddyfile with the text below. Replace your-name.duckdns.org with your domain, and put one header line in for each person's key (the example shows two people). One line per person is how you add or remove people later.

your-name.duckdns.org {
	request_body {
		max_size 64KB
	}

	@allowed {
		header Authorization "Bearer PUT-FIRST-KEY-HERE"
		header Authorization "Bearer PUT-SECOND-KEY-HERE"
		method GET POST
		path /v1/chat/completions /v1/models
	}

	handle @allowed {
		reverse_proxy 127.0.0.1:11434 {
			header_up Host 127.0.0.1:11434
		}
	}

	handle {
		respond "Not found" 404
	}
}

What it does: only people with a listed key get through, and only to the two things Reword needs. Everyone else gets "Not found". The header_up Host line is needed because Ollama refuses requests that arrive under another name.

Windows 11

Make a folder, open Notepad to create the file in it, and save it as C:\caddy\Caddyfile (choose "All files" so it does not become Caddyfile.txt):

mkdir C:\caddy
notepad C:\caddy\Caddyfile

Linux

sudo nano /etc/caddy/Caddyfile

Replace everything in the file with the text above, save (Ctrl+O, Enter) and exit (Ctrl+X).

B6. Start Caddy

Windows 11

caddy run --config C:\caddy\Caddyfile

Leave that window open. Caddy stops when you close it. To start it automatically with Windows, see Caddy's Windows service instructions.

Linux

sudo systemctl restart caddy
sudo systemctl status caddy

The first start gets your certificate, which takes a few seconds. If it complains about "timeout" or "firewall", the router forward or your provider is blocking port 80 or 443.

B7. Test it

From a computer or a phone on mobile data (not your Wi-Fi), open https://your-name.duckdns.org/v1/models in a browser. You should see Not found and a padlock. That means it is protected. Now check a key works (replace both names):

Windows 11

curl.exe -H "Authorization: Bearer YOUR-KEY" https://your-name.duckdns.org/v1/models

Linux

curl -H "Authorization: Bearer YOUR-KEY" https://your-name.duckdns.org/v1/models

It should list your models. Then go on to Step 4.

Option C: at home only, with a certificate

Reword from Google Play only accepts https://, and a phone only trusts a certificate for a domain name. So even at home you need a domain name. The trick: the name points to your computer's private home address, which only works when your phone is on your home Wi-Fi. Away from home the name leads nowhere, so Reword will not connect (that is what you asked for).

Read this first, and remember: do this at your own risk.
  • It only works on your home Wi-Fi. If you want it to work away from home too, use Option A.
  • A few routers block names that point to a private address ("DNS rebind protection"). If Step C5 fails for that reason, turn that setting off in the router, or use Option A.
  • Your computer's home address must not change. In your router, look for "reserve this address" or "DHCP reservation" for your computer.
  • Here Ollama stays listening only to its own computer. Do not do Option A's step A1.
  • Anyone on your Wi-Fi who knows the name can use your AI. That is usually fine at home.

C1. A free domain name with DuckDNS

  1. Go to duckdns.org, sign in, type a name (for example my-reword) and click add domain. Copy your token from the top of the page. Treat it like a password.
  2. Find your computer's home address:

    Windows 11

    ipconfig

    Look for IPv4 Address, for example 192.168.1.20.

    Linux

    hostname -I

    Use the first address, for example 192.168.1.20.

  3. On the DuckDNS page, put that address in the current ip box next to your name and click update ip.

C2. Open the secure port on your computer

Windows 11

In Terminal opened as administrator:

New-NetFirewallRule -DisplayName "Reword Caddy" -Direction Inbound -Protocol TCP -LocalPort 443 -Profile Private -Action Allow

Linux

sudo ufw allow 443/tcp

(Only if you use ufw.)

Do not forward any ports on your router.

C3. Install Caddy with the DuckDNS add-on

Install Caddy exactly as in B4 above. Then add the DuckDNS add-on, which lets Caddy prove the name is yours without opening any port:

Windows 11 (Terminal as administrator)

caddy add-package github.com/caddy-dns/duckdns

Linux

sudo caddy add-package github.com/caddy-dns/duckdns
sudo systemctl restart caddy

If that command is not available, download a Caddy that includes the duckdns add-on from caddyserver.com/download and use it in place of the one you installed.

C4. The Caddy settings file

Make a file named Caddyfile with this text. Replace my-reword with your DuckDNS name and YOUR-DUCKDNS-TOKEN with your token.

my-reword.duckdns.org {
	tls {
		dns duckdns YOUR-DUCKDNS-TOKEN
	}
	reverse_proxy 127.0.0.1:11434 {
		header_up Host 127.0.0.1:11434
	}
}

Windows 11

mkdir C:\caddy
notepad C:\caddy\Caddyfile

Save with "All files" so it does not become Caddyfile.txt.

Linux

sudo nano /etc/caddy/Caddyfile

Replace everything in the file with the text above, save (Ctrl+O, Enter) and exit (Ctrl+X).

C5. Start Caddy and test

Windows 11

caddy run --config C:\caddy\Caddyfile

Leave the window open. Caddy stops when you close it.

Linux

sudo systemctl restart caddy
sudo systemctl status caddy

The first start takes a few seconds while Caddy gets the certificate. Then, with your phone on your home Wi-Fi, open https://my-reword.duckdns.org in the phone's browser. It should say Ollama is running with a padlock. With the phone on mobile data it should not connect. Then go on to Step 4.

Option D: other people, with Tailscale sharing

Read this first, and remember: do this at your own risk.
  • Each person you share with can reach your whole computer over Tailscale, not only Ollama, unless you limit them with Tailscale's access rules. Share only with people you trust, or use a spare computer for this.
  • Each person needs a Tailscale account of their own. The free plan has limits on users and devices; check Tailscale's current limits before you invite people.
  • Do Option A first (A1 and A2). This option only adds the sharing.

D1. Get Option A working for yourself

Follow Option A above until your own phone can open your https://...ts.net address and sees Ollama is running.

D2. Share your computer with a person

  1. Open login.tailscale.com/admin/machines.
  2. Find your computer, click the three dots at the end of its row and choose Share.
  3. Create an invite link and send it to the person.

D3. What the person does

  1. Install the Tailscale app on their phone (Google Play) and sign in with their own free account.
  2. Open your invite link and accept it. Your computer now shows up for them.
  3. Keep Tailscale turned on while using Reword.
  4. Use your full address in Reword (the https://...ts.net one from Option A). They leave Server key empty.

Then go on to Step 4. It should work the same way as for you, but I have not been able to test sharing across two accounts, so if the address does not load for them, check Tailscale's own sharing instructions.

D4. Taking access away

In the same Machines page, open your computer's menu and remove the share for that person. They lose access at once.

Step 4: Connect Reword

  1. Open Reword and go to step 1, Which AI should do the rewriting?, and choose My own server.
  2. In Server address put your address: the https://...ts.net one from Option A, or https://your-name.duckdns.org from Option B, or https://my-reword.duckdns.org from Option C (no port). With Option D, people use your full https://...ts.net address.
  3. In Server key: leave it empty for Options A, C and D; for Option B put the key you made for that person.
  4. Open Advanced and choose your model from the model pull-down (the one you downloaded).
  5. Tap Test. When it says Connected, you are done.

With Option A, Tailscale has to be turned on on the phone, also when you are at home. With Option C, Reword only works while the phone is on your home Wi-Fi.

If something goes wrong

  • "Forbidden" or 403 (Option A): Ollama is still listening only to its own computer. Repeat A1 and restart Ollama.
  • Test cannot connect: Option A: check Tailscale is on in the phone app, the computer is awake, and the address matches tailscale serve status exactly. Option B: check Caddy is running and the key is right.
  • Option D: the person cannot connect: check they accepted your invite, Tailscale is turned on in their phone app, and the address is exactly the one from tailscale serve status on your computer.
  • Option C cannot connect: check the phone is on your home Wi-Fi, the computer is awake, the DuckDNS address still matches ipconfig or hostname -I, and your router's "DNS rebind protection" is not blocking the name.
  • Option B certificate "timeout" or "firewall": the router forward or your provider is blocking port 80 or 443.
  • The model is missing from the pull-down: run ollama list on the computer to see what is installed.
  • The first rewrite is slow: the computer is loading the model. Later ones are faster. Ollama unloads the model after a few idle minutes by default.
  • Too slow overall: choose a smaller model in Step 2.

Managing it later

Option B: adding and removing people

  • Add: make a key (Step 4), add a header Authorization "Bearer KEY" line to the Caddyfile, and reload:

    Windows 11

    caddy reload --config C:\caddy\Caddyfile

    Linux

    sudo systemctl reload caddy
  • Remove: delete that person's line and reload. Their key stops working at once, and nobody else is affected.

On Windows, caddy reload may report that the admin endpoint is unavailable if you did not start Caddy with the same settings; in that case close the Caddy window and run B6 again.

Option A: stopping it

To stop sharing with your phone: tailscale serve reset (on Linux, with sudo). To make Ollama listen only on its own computer again, undo A1.
